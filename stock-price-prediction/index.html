01 / OVERVIEW

Can machine learning outperform statistical forecasts?

A multi-model comparison of traditional and ML-based closing-price prediction.

Stock closing prices are difficult to forecast because time-series relationships can change over time and are affected by nonlinear dynamics. This work compares simple historical-price forecasting rules against a diverse collection of regressors and neural-network models.

The evaluation uses Tesla and Apple stock-price datasets and reports mean squared error (MSE) and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE). Machine-learning models consume the daily opening, highest and lowest prices to predict the closing price.

Statistical baselines

SMA averages recent closing prices; WMA assigns different weights; exponential smoothing gives recent observations greater influence; the naïve predictor uses the previous close.

Machine-learning models

Linear, Lasso and Ridge regression, KNN, SVR, Random Forest, single-layer perceptron, multi-layer perceptron and LSTM learn mappings from price features to the closing price.

Performance metricsMean Squared Error (MSE) and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)
MSE = (1/n) Σ (yi − ŷi)²
MAPE = (100/n) Σ |(yi − ŷi) / yi|

Both metrics are lower-is-better. MSE penalizes large absolute errors more strongly; MAPE expresses relative errors as a percentage. As the target is the daily close and the inputs include same-day open/high/low values, results should not be read as a pure previous-day-only forecasting experiment.

05 / DISCUSSION

What the model comparison does and does not establish

Main finding

Neural-network models, especially LSTM and selected MLP configurations, outperform the traditional moving-average baselines on the reported Tesla and Apple evaluations.

Evaluation caveat

Because the learning models use same-day open/high/low to predict closing price, their informational inputs differ from baselines operating on prior closes. The scores do not by themselves demonstrate an investable, next-day trading advantage.

For a modern extension, align all methods to the same forecast horizon and information cutoff, use walk-forward temporal evaluation, include transaction costs and compare performance under different market regimes. These are recommendations for future research, not reported tests in the 2019 paper.
