Memory vs. Attention: Understanding Their Roles in Deep Knowledge Tracing Architectures

Indronil Bhattacharjee

LAK 2025 · Poster Track · Dublin, Ireland · March 3–7, 2025

A comparative review of memory-based and attention-based knowledge tracing models examines how architecture affects the representation of learner history and the prediction of future performance.

Companion proceedings ↗View proceedings PDF ↗
Research question

How do KT models represent student history?

Deep knowledge tracing models rely on different mechanisms to represent student knowledge, but their architectural tradeoffs are not always clear when choosing a model for personalized learning.

Approach

How the work is structured

01

Review memory-based KT

Discuss DKVMN and SKVMN, including persistent memory states and mechanisms for tracking concept-level history.

02

Review attention-based KT

Discuss SAKT and AKT, including adaptive weighting of historical interactions and context-aware representations.

03

Compare published results

Summarize reported AUC results across ASSISTments datasets and connect the comparison to cold-start research questions.

Highlights

What the paper contributes

4Core architectures compared
ASSISTmentsDataset family discussed
PosterLAK25 Companion Proceedings

Attention-based models are competitive across the summarized benchmark comparisons, while memory-based approaches offer explicit concept-state representations. The poster reviews prior results rather than claiming a new head-to-head controlled benchmark.
