01 / OVERVIEW

Practice social skills in realistic branching situations

A demonstrated system architecture for scenario authoring, interactive rehearsal and immediate and delayed feedback.

Conventional instruction about communication may not give learners enough opportunities to rehearse difficult, emotionally charged interactions. GLOSS (Guided Learning for Optimizing Soft Skills) combines an instructor-controlled scenario graph and LLM-generated dialogue so that students can practice realistic exchanges and receive feedback.

Its central design decision is to keep an instructor in control of objectives and story structure while allowing a generative model to extend scenario paths when learners respond in unexpected ways.

Instructor-in-the-loop

Create or edit branching scenarios without writing code, including templates, direct scripting and prompting an LLM.

Learner-in-the-loop

Practice via a conversational avatar, receive immediate response-level feedback and review a path through the scenario afterward.

03 / METHOD

How the LLM works with the narrative graph

Instructor-authored structure

An instructor creates a scenario such as responding to an unhappy customer. Nodes represent situations and directed edges encode the possible next situations depending on the learner’s response. The graph offers a persistent structure for assessment and content revision.

Dynamic expansion at runtime

When a student response does not correspond to a prepared edge, GPT-4 can generate a new response-conditioned transition and add it to the graph. The simulator then produces appropriate avatar dialogue. Feedback is generated using a separate LLM prompt.

What the AAAI demonstration establishes

The paper presents GLOSS as a functioning demonstration and explains how its components support instructor co-creation, student rehearsal and assessment. It describes GPT-4-based components, structured narrative scenarios and dynamic branch generation. These are system capabilities, not effect sizes.

The paper does not report controlled learner outcome scores, a sample size for evaluation, learning gains or a quantitative comparison against another tutoring tool. User studies are listed as future work; no performance numbers have been invented for this page.
05 / DISCUSSION

Design contributions and future validation

Contribution 1: accessible authoring

Instructors without software development expertise can create interactive social-skills training scenarios and reshape them around training goals.

Contribution 2: open-ended rehearsal

The system balances a planned scenario graph with LLM flexibility, making unexpected but plausible student responses less likely to terminate practice.

Contribution 3: two-stage feedback

Students receive immediate guidance during rehearsal and a visual review of their chosen scenario path afterward.

Future evaluation

The paper calls for user studies, better avatar responses, improvements to feedback and a more effective balance between scripted and open interactions.
