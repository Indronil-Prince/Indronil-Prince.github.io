01 / OVERVIEW

Recognizing handwritten Bangla digits at scale

Classification of offline handwritten numerals with a custom CNN.

Handwritten Bengali numerals vary substantially across writers in stroke shape, scale, orientation and pen pressure. This project studies whether a compact custom convolutional neural network can learn digit-discriminative visual patterns without manual feature engineering.

The study begins with the Ekush Bangla Handwritten Data — Numerals dataset, standardizes the input images, augments the training collection and classifies each image into one of ten digits (০–৯).

Convolution + pooling

The two convolutional blocks learn local shapes and stroke patterns. Max-pooling reduces spatial resolution and creates some tolerance to small position changes.

Classification head

A 1,000-neuron fully connected layer maps spatial features into a discriminative representation. The final 10-way softmax estimates the probability of each digit.

The paper identifies sparse categorical cross-entropy as the training objective and stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for optimization. It describes implementation with TensorFlow/Keras on a Kaggle GPU runtime.

05 / DISCUSSION

Takeaways and limitations

What the experiments demonstrate

A relatively straightforward convolution + pooling network can recognize the ten Bangla numerals with high reported accuracy. Performance on the separately collected 300-image sample suggests useful robustness beyond the main curated dataset.

What remains open

The sample evaluation is small, and the publication does not provide a writer-independent verification protocol for the augmented split. More robust testing would isolate writers, source images and acquisition conditions.

Future direction: extending digit recognition to the complete Bangla alphabet, compound characters and ligatures, as proposed in the paper.
