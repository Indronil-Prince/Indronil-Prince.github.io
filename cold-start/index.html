Cold Start Problem: An Experimental Study of Knowledge Tracing Models with New Students

How well can knowledge tracing models predict a completely new student's responses when only a few early interactions are available? We evaluate three influential architectures under a realistic new-student protocol.

01 / OVERVIEW

What happens when a student is brand new?

Knowledge tracing predicts students' future correctness from their prior interactions with learning activities. But a newcomer has little interaction history. Models that perform well on familiar students may not generalize equally well at the start of a new student's learning journey.

Our study isolates the new-student cold-start problem. Instead of mixing a student's early history into training, we train only on historical students and evaluate on completely held-out students. This better reflects the challenge of predicting for a learner who has just joined an intelligent tutoring system.

“Being accurate after a long history is not the same as being accurate from the beginning.

03 / FINDINGS

What the comparison revealed

Evaluation spans three ASSISTments datasets and three widely used deep KT architectures.

EARLY INTERACTIONS
A difficult start

All three models face challenges when making predictions with little student-specific evidence. Cold-start evaluation exposes this weakness.

Short histories are especially challenging.
AS HISTORY GROWS
Gradual improvement

Prediction performance generally improves as more interactions become available. SAKT shows comparatively strong early accuracy, but it also has limitations.

More evidence helps, but does not erase the problem.
→
Research direction: learn to adapt quickly

The findings motivate methods built specifically for new-student generalization and few-shot adaptation. Our subsequent MAML-KT project explores this direction.
