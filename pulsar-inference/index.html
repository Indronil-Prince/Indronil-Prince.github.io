01 / OVERVIEW

The inverse problem: from pulses to physical parameters

Learn a useful signal representation, retrieve promising parameter configurations and refine them efficiently.

Different neutron-star magnetic field configurations can yield very similar observed light curves. As a result, inferring the physical configuration means estimating a posterior distribution rather than simply returning one best-fit point.

Conventional MCMC methods explore these distributions but require substantial time and computation. This work proposes a more efficient approximation that first finds plausible parameter regions globally and then sharpens each region locally using a simulator.

p(θ | x*) ∝ p(θ) p(x* | θ)

Why not direct regression?

A single prediction can overlook physically distinct configurations that produce almost the same light curve. Retrieval preserves multiple possible explanations.

What is preserved?

The method aims to retain multimodal structure and parameter degeneracies, not to provide asymptotically exact Bayesian samples.

DATA

Input

A pulsar light curve sampled over 64 phase bins; each simulation has an associated 11-dimensional physical parameter vector.

REPRESENTATION

Multi-level embedding

Temporal average pooling produces a 256-D encoder feature and 512-D bottleneck descriptor, concatenated into 768-D.

SEARCH

Retrieval

The top 20,000 embedding matches are re-ranked in physical light-curve space using MdNSE, leaving K = 4,000 seeds.

03 / METHOD

Masked representation learning and adaptive refinement

Masked U-Net pretraining

A one-dimensional U-Net learns to recover missing pulse bins using the surrounding signal. The encoder builds a multi-scale representation with channel widths 64 → 128 → 256 → 512. Reconstruction loss is calculated on masked positions; AdamW training uses 10 million simulated curves and 200 epochs.

Lmask = Σt mt(x̂t − xt)² / Σt mt

Simulator-guided block hill climbing

Each retrieved seed is refined by perturbing a subset of P parameters, evaluating signed candidates through the forward simulator, accepting an improving move and adaptively changing the step size. The objective is Poisson negative log-likelihood (NLL).

L(θ) = (1/T) Σt [ Ft(θ) − x*t log(Ft(θ) + ε) ]

Joint changes to correlated physical parameters help traverse a complex posterior landscape while each retrieved initialization remains a local mode-specific starting point.

The result is an empirical approximate posterior, not a guarantee of exact posterior sampling or full Bayesian uncertainty calibration.

Secondary validation

The paper also applies the approach to Ba-133 gamma-ray photopeak inference, a four-parameter spectroscopy task. With 10 million synthetic spectra, retrieval exceeded 0.999 cosine similarity; subsequent refinement lowered mean NLL from −9227.6425 to −9228.7829.

These results concern the reported pulsar and spectroscopy evaluations. They are not a universal latency or posterior-fidelity guarantee for new observations.
05 / DISCUSSION

What the findings establish

Key contribution

The global retrieval stage finds many plausible solutions; the local simulator stage improves each candidate without relying on MCMC to explore the entire parameter space.

Important limitations

The approximation depends on simulation-bank coverage, learned similarity and a forward simulator. Its local search does not provide the asymptotic sampling guarantees of MCMC.

For PSR J0030+0451, the retrieved and refined samples reproduce multimodal posterior features and predict light curves that align with the MCMC baseline. Multi-level embeddings provide better pulse-morphology retrieval than bottleneck-only embeddings or cosine comparison on raw signals.
