Accelerating Posterior Inference from Pulsar Light Curves via Learned Latent Representations and Local Simulator-Guided Optimization

Farhana Taiyebah, Abu Bucker Siddik, Indronil Bhattacharjee, Diane Oyen, Soumi De, Greg Olmschenk, Constantinos Kalapotharakos

AI4Physics @ ICML 2026 · Seoul, South Korea · July 6–11, 2026

A learned representation of pulsar light curves helps retrieve candidate physical parameters, while a forward simulator guides local refinement to approximate complex posterior distributions much faster than traditional MCMC.

Read paper ↗Download PDF ↗Preprint DOI ↗
Research question

Fast inference for neutron-star physics

Estimating pulsar parameters from observed light curves is an expensive inverse problem. Traditional Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods can capture uncertainty but require substantial computation.

Approach

How the work is structured

01

Learn the representation

Pretrain a masked U-Net to reconstruct light curves and produce informative latent embeddings.

02

Retrieve candidate parameters

Match an observed light curve to a bank of simulated curves in the learned embedding space.

03

Refine with physics

Use simulator-guided local hill-climbing to move retrieved candidates toward higher-likelihood regions.

Highlights

What the paper contributes

120×Faster inference
24 h → 12 minReported runtime reduction
PSR J0030+0451Observed pulsar evaluated

On NICER observations of PSR J0030+0451, the approximate posterior closely matched MCMC estimates while substantially reducing computation. The method is an efficient approximation, not an exact Bayesian sampler.
